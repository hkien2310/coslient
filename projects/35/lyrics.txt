I came home wearing the whole day wrong,
In the bend of my back, in the drag of my palms.
The stairwell held its breath for me,
Like even the walls knew what the hours had been.

I turned the key and the lock gave way.
A little bowl rang bright in the dim hallway.
One thin note, then another one stayed,
As if the room had heard my name all day.

My shoes by the threshold, heavy with street,
Full of old weather and city heat.
I set them down, and the floor went still,
Like it knew tired things and always will.


My coat slipped loose from my shoulder bone,
Missed the hook, found the chair like home.
And the chair leaned in with its patient frame,
Like it already knew the shape of my strain.


The doorway keeps what I can’t carry.
The lamp turns gold, the air turns slow.
The chair already knows my shape.
I set the day down and let it go.

My shoes are full of city weather.
The walls take one long breath with me.
I come in hard, I come in frayed,
And leave here soft enough to sleep.


The kettle found its silver throat,
A low warm hum, one trembling note.
The dark in the corners folded small,
Like someone took it down from the wall.

The table waited without a sound.
The window wore the evening down.
A yellow line across the floor
Looked almost kind, and then looked more.

Not miracle, not flame, not sign,
Just a room behaving like it might be mine.
The kind of mercy houses keep,
In boards that settle and lights that breathe.


Outside, the world had all its edges,
Its bright demands, its iron pace.
But here the floorboards took my footsteps
And rubbed the hardness from their shape.


The doorway keeps what I can’t carry.
The lamp turns gold, the air turns slow.
The chair already knows my shape.
I set the day down and let it go.

My shoes are full of city weather.
The walls take one long breath with me.
I come in hard, I come in frayed,
And leave here soft enough to sleep.


Maybe grace is never grander
Than a bowl, a chair, a kitchen light.
Maybe peace is something domestic,
Crooked a little, but exactly right.

Maybe love, when it lasts long enough,
Learns the weight of your silence too.
Learns your tiredness by the doorway,
And waits each night to carry it for you.


The doorway keeps what I can’t carry.
The lamp burns low, the room turns deep.
The chair already knows my shape.
It holds the parts I could not keep.

My shoes are full of broken weather.
The walls go still and breathe with me.
I come in worn, I come in late,
And leave here soft enough to dream.


Tonight, the house can keep it.
