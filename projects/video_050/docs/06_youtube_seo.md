DESCRIPTION:
She kneels in the cold morning dirt to plant seeds she won't live to see bloom. This is a song about the quiet legacies we leave for the people coming after us.

I wrote this piece after watching an older neighbor tend her front garden every single spring. It struck me that many of the saplings she watered would easily outlive her. She wasn't planting them to enjoy the shade herself. She was planting because the ground needed seeds, and because someone else would eventually walk down that sidewalk. That small act of quiet, everyday generosity felt important. It felt like something worth remembering.

The song tells it better than I can. Here are the complete lyrics to read as you listen:

This arrangement rests on a fingerpicked acoustic guitar and a gentle cello that builds as the story shifts from the grandmother to the little girl. We brought in a rolling accordion to give it a pastoral folk feeling, similar to an old countryside waltz. The vocal is an unhurried alto—someone who has lived long enough to accept the passage of time without rushing it.

This video was made using AI-generated imagery, shaped specifically to feel like a warm, handcrafted storybook miniature rather than a digital spectacle. Every frame was positioned to capture that early morning golden hour and the smooth, tactile texture. Tools were used, but the feeling of quiet love came first.
