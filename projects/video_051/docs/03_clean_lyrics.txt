I drove all night to leave the concrete road,
And parked the car beside the coastal clay.
I took my heavy boots off in the dirt,
To see if water washes off the day.
The morning air is biting at my face,
The tide is pulling back to leave a space.

The icy water washes round my feet,
And makes my heavy shoulders finally drop.
I hear the rhythm of the open wide,
The only thing that doesn't need to stop.

The water holds your weight,
Without asking for your name.
It takes the heavy things away,
And leaves you standing in the rain.
The water holds your heavy bones,
And washes quietly against the stones.

An older man is sitting down the beach,
Repairing broken netting by the fire.
He doesn't look at me or say a word,
He only pulls a piece of rusted wire.
He's lived his fifty years upon this sand,
The salt is written deep inside his hand.

He looks up at the endless indigo,
And goes back to the work he has to do.
He knows the water is the only home,
And lets it do the holding for me too.

The water holds your weight,
Without asking for your name.
It takes the heavy things away,
And washes everything the same.
The water holds your heavy bones,
And settles quietly against the stones.

We are strangers on the morning beach.
But we are breathing the exact same air.
The ocean doesn't care where we have been.
It only meets us when we're standing there.

The water holds our weight!
Without asking for our name!
It takes the heavy things away,
And washes everything the same!
The water holds our heavy bones,
And settles quietly against the stones.

We leave our boots upon the sand.
The water doesn't ask.
