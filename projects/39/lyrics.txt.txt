There’s a shop on a narrow corner
Where the evening settles slow
People bring the worn-out pieces
Only old things know

A cracked-up frame, a wedding cup
A letter brown with time
A watch that quit on Sunday noon
A bell that lost its chime

He lays them on the wooden bench
And turns them in his hands
Like every scratch and faded mark
Is something he understands

He doesn’t ask what you forgot
He doesn’t need the name
He only listens to the dust
And lets it speak again

What do the old things know
That we let go
What do they carry
When the years move slow
A little song
A weathered coat
A table ring
A folded note
When the heart forgets
Where the warm years go
Ask what the old things know

A woman brings a radio
That hasn’t played in years
He turns the dial carefully
Past the static and the tears

Then somewhere under all that noise
A melody comes through
The kitchen floor, her mother’s voice
A summer afternoon

Not a ghost, not yesterday
Not time returning home
Just the sound of being loved
Before she felt alone

What do the old things know
That we let go
What do they carry
When the years move slow
A little song
A weathered coat
A table ring
A folded note
When the heart forgets
Where the warm years go
Ask what the old things know

Maybe nothing really leaves
The way we think it does
Maybe every ordinary thing
Remembers being loved

Maybe hands that held us once
Are closer than they seem
In the cup, the clock, the photograph
The button, book, and seam

What do the old things know
That we let go
What do they carry
When the years move slow
A little laugh
A Sunday coat
A table ring
A folded note
When the heart forgets
Where the warm years go
Ask what the old things know

So bring the little broken things
You almost threw away
Some memories don’t need much
To find their voice again
